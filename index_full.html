Video Models Reason Early:
Exploiting Plan Commitment for Maze Solving

Kaleb Newman  ·  Tyler Zhu  ·  Olga Russakovsky

Princeton University

📄 Paper 📚 arXiv 💻 Code

Abstract

Video diffusion models exhibit emergent reasoning capabilities like solving mazes and puzzles, yet little is understood about how they reason during generation. We study the internal planning dynamics of video models using 2D maze-solving as a controlled testbed and reveal two findings. First, early plan commitment: video diffusion models commit to a high-level motion plan within the first few denoising steps, after which further denoising alters visual details but not the underlying trajectory. Second, path length—not obstacle density—is the dominant predictor of maze difficulty, with a sharp failure threshold at 12 steps. To demonstrate the practical benefits of these findings, we introduce Chaining with Early Planning (ChEaP), which only spends compute on seeds with promising early plans and chains them together to tackle complex mazes. ChEaP improves accuracy from 7% to 67% on long-horizon mazes and by 2.5× overall on hard tasks in Frozen Lake and VR-Bench across Wan2.2-14B and HunyuanVideo-1.5.

Early Plan Commitment

Video diffusion models commit to a trajectory plan within the first few denoising steps. Later steps refine visual fidelity—the route itself is already decided.

Trajectory convergence between intermediate and final predictions. Step 5 already reaches 93% on 4×4 mazes.
Re-noising at each step produces nearly identical trajectories, far below the cross-seed baseline. Plans are encoded in the initial noise.
Since trajectories are locked in early, inference-time compute should be spent screening more candidates early rather than fully denoising every seed.

Screening Diverse Candidate Plans

Different noise seeds produce strikingly different motion plans on the same maze. Our method, Early Planning Beam Search (EPBS), partially denoises many candidates, scores their early trajectory plans, and only fully renders the most promising ones. Below, we visualize the diverse candidate trajectories sampled for a single maze—failed paths appear as gray silhouettes, successful trajectories in vivid color.

ChEaP: Chaining with Early Planning

Early Planning Beam Search (EPBS) exploits early plan commitment to efficiently allocate inference-time compute. Instead of fully denoising every candidate, EPBS screens early trajectory plans and only invests in promising seeds.

1

Partial Denoising

Denoise N candidates for only τ steps to reveal early plans.

2

Early Screening

Score each candidate's progress and constraint violations.

3

Beam Selection

Fully denoise only the top-K most promising seeds.

4

Chaining

Chain sequential generations for long-horizon mazes.

EPBS matches or exceeds best-of-N accuracy while using significantly fewer function evaluations across all maze sizes.

Our findings generalize across architectures. Both Wan2.2-14B (40-step flow matching) and HunyuanVideo-1.5 (8-step distilled) exhibit early plan commitment.

Constraint violations dominate on small mazes; horizon limitations take over as mazes grow. Path length—not obstacle density—is the dominant predictor of difficulty.
